# The session script: five people over 70, from "here is a link" to "call your grandson"

For WP-92 (early check, three people, now) and WP-43 (the real test, five
people). The owner recruits (`RECRUITING-NOTE.md`); the observer runs this.
Sheets: `SCORING-SHEET.md`. Consent: `CONSENT.md`. Equipment and set-up:
`RUNNING-IT.md`. What we expect to see, so that we can be surprised:
`docs/research/people-walkthrough-2026-10.md`.

## 1. The pass rule (change it only by editing this section)

**The product passes when 4 of 5 people pass, and a person passes when all of
these are true.**

1. **Unaided** on T1, T2 and T3. Unaided means level 0 on the aid ladder (4.3):
   nobody spoke to her except to say the words in this script, nobody touched
   the mouse or keyboard, nobody phoned, and nothing was looked up for her. She
   may use everything AurOS gives a person: the website, the guide she printed
   herself, the Help button.
2. **Under 60 minutes** on Clock A: from the moment the observer puts the link in
   front of her (T1 start) to the moment she sees and hears her grandson on the
   call (T3 end). The computer's own waiting counts. It is her hour.
3. **Unaided on T4 and T6** (bigger words, back to Windows). They are outside
   Clock A. T6 is a safety task: a product she cannot undo has not passed.
4. **Zero data loss** (section 7), on her own computer.

T5 (a wish) is scored and reported but **outside the pass rule while Wishes is
not in the image (decision D14)**. It joins the rule the day Wishes ships.

Nobody is told whether she passed. If she asks: "You helped us a lot."

## 2. Before anyone comes

- Read `RUNNING-IT.md`; set up the stations; run the **dress rehearsal** (4.5)
  on a younger colleague. Fix the script, not the colleague.
- Screening is done (`RECRUITING-NOTE.md`): Windows version, free room, laptop or
  desktop, Wi-Fi or cable, antivirus name, BitLocker on or off, administrator or
  not, which email she reads on this computer, a photo she would know, her
  grandson's (or granddaughter's) first name. A computer AurOS refuses is
  not a wasted session: use a lab computer for the same person and write
  "refused PC" in the sheet.
- Two people: **the observer** (speaks the script, nothing else, sits behind and
  to her left, not in her line of sight) and **the scribe** (stopwatch and the
  sheet). One person can do both with the screen recording running; the sheet
  is then filled from the recording within the hour.
- A third person plays the **grandson**: on a phone or laptop in another room,
  logged in to the call, camera on, waiting. They know the first name she
  will say and answer as a grandson would.
- On the table: the sheets, a pen, two glasses of water, tissues, a printed
  copy of CONSENT in 18-point type, the thank-you, a clock she can see
  (cover it if it worries her; she may not want a clock).
- Her chair, her lighting, her own glasses, her own mouse. Never change her
  computer's settings before she starts, even the cursor size.

## 3. The words (say them exactly; they are the whole script)

**Welcome (before recording).** "Thank you for coming. We are trying out a
computer program. We are testing the program, not you: nothing you do can be
wrong, and if something is confusing, that is our mistake and it is exactly
what we need to find. While you try, I won't be able to help you, because
we need to see what happens when nobody is there. That is not me being
rude. You can stop whenever you like, for any reason, and you don't have to
say why. May I show you the form?" Read the consent form with her, aloud,
slowly. She signs, or does not. If she does not, thank her and stop.

**Before T1.** "I'd like to record the screen so that I don't have to
write everything down. May I? [yes or no on the form]. If you feel like
saying what you are thinking while you work, please do. You don't have to."

**T1, "Get AurOS onto this computer".** Put the link in front of her the way
she normally gets links (the default: an email, in the mailbox she reads on
this computer; `PHONE-LINK` variant: a text to her own phone). Say: "Someone
told you about a free program called AurOS. It gives a Windows computer a
new way of working, and here is the link. I'd like you to get AurOS onto
this computer. Take all the time you like." Start Clock A.

**T2, "Find a photo".** (When she has answered the first-start question.)
"AurOS is on the computer now. Please find a photo of [what she said at
screening, in her words]." Do not say where.

**T3, "Make a video call to your grandson".** "Please make a video call to
[name]. He is waiting for you." Stop Clock A when she sees and hears him.

**T4, "Make the words bigger".** "The words on this computer are a little
small for you. Please make them bigger, the way you would like them." Then,
when she has done something: "Please open the internet and show me a page." (so
that the words are measured in the program she actually reads).

**T5, "Make a wish".** (Only on a build with Wishes.) "This computer can do
things when you tell it. Please make a wish: you would like it to remind you to
ring your sister at nine."

**T6, "Get back to Windows".** "Imagine you have decided this is not for you.
Please put your Windows back, the way it was."

**Close.** "That's everything. Thank you. You did something very useful
for us." Then the interview (section 8).

**What you never say**: "good", "that's right", "no, not that", "try the
button on the left", "it's easy", "almost". No sound of encouragement or
doubt, no sighs, no nodding. Look at the sheet, not at her hands.

## 4. While she works

### 4.1 Silence

You are a piece of furniture. She will ask you questions ("Is this right?",
"What do I do now?"). Say, kindly and the same every time: "What would you do
if I weren't here?" If she asks for help outright: "I'm not able to help
with this part. Would you like to carry on, or try the next thing?" Write the
question down word for word. Every question is a finding.

### 4.2 What the scribe records, at every step in `SCORING-SHEET.md`

- the time on Clock A when she starts and ends the step;
- a **hesitation** when nothing has happened for more than 10 seconds: tally
  it, and write what was on the screen;
- every **question** she asks, verbatim;
- every **error**: a wrong press, a miss, a double press, a press that undid
  something;
- every **aid** (4.3) with its level;
- her **words** when she reads or mutters something: they are the most
  valuable thing in the sheet;
- **where her hands go**: mouse, keyboard, or the power button.

### 4.3 The aid ladder

An aid is anything beyond the words in section 3. A task is **unaided** only if
no aid at all was given.

| Level | The observer | Counts as |
|---|---|---|
| A0 | nothing | unaided |
| A1 | asks a neutral question: "What do you see?" "What would you like to do?" | aided |
| A2 | points at an area: "Look at the bottom of the screen." | aided |
| A3 | names the thing: "Press Next." | aided |
| A4 | does it for her | not done |

Offer an aid only when she has been stuck on one step for **5 minutes**, or at
once if she is distressed. At 5 minutes: "Would you like to carry on, or
shall we try the next thing?" At 10 minutes, if she wants it: A1, then A2,
then A3, each only if she asks. Never A4 unless she asks for it or the data
is at risk (4.4).

### 4.4 The two stops

1. **The power button.** During the restart, the dark gap, the staging screens
   and the "putting Windows back" screen, keep your hand ready. If hers goes
   to the power button, or she says she will switch it off, say, once, calmly:
   "Please wait. It is working and it is safe. I will tell you when it is done."
   Write **POWER ATTEMPT**, the time, and the words on the screen. This does not
   end the session. It counts as an aid (so she cannot pass), and it is the most
   important line in the sheet: it is how a person loses her files.
2. **Her.** If she is tired, upset, or unwell: stop, offer water, sit with her.
   "We can stop here." Write the time and why. Never push on to finish a
   task. No result is worth more than her afternoon.

### 4.5 Time caps (so that nobody is kept sitting)

T1 75 minutes, T2 10, T3 15, T4 5, T5 10, T6 15. At the cap, say: "That's
very useful. Shall we go on to the next one?" and mark the task **not
finished**. T1 and T6 leave her computer in a state: if she has not finished
them, finish them for her (A4), explain it to her, and write exactly what you did.
Her computer is never left half done.

### 4.6 Breaks

A 10-minute break after T3 (or at any request). Take the recording off during
the break. Let her walk about.

## 6. Two modes

**Mode E, early check (WP-92), now.** The wizard is the click-through simulation
(`docs/usability/wizard-sim/index.html`); AurOS is a virtual or spare machine that
is already installed and at the first-run question. Steps 1.01 to 1.06 are skipped
(start at the sim's first screen), the dark gap and staging screen are
the sim's own (a black screen of 25 seconds, then the staging picture), and T6 is
run on the AurOS machine. Nothing on her own computer is touched: there is no data
loss to measure, so the pass rule is **not** evaluated; the sheets are.
Three people. The findings go to B, C, D and K before the wizard is frozen.

**Mode F, full test (WP-43).** Her own computer, a real install from the real
published installer, all six tasks, the pass rule. Five people, after WP-33's
real-PC campaign and after WP-30 (boot fallback), WP-12, WP-17 and WP-26 have
landed (`docs/PLAN-END-TO-END.md`, section 2, WP-43).

## 7. Data safety, measured

Her files are the only thing that matters more than the result. In Mode F:

**Before T1, on her computer, by the observer (never by her), with her
permission:**
1. Offer a copy: "Would you like us to copy your photos and documents onto
   this drive first, so they are safe whatever happens? We give you the drive."
   Copy Desktop, Documents and Pictures to the drive; compare the counts.
2. Write the baseline into `baseline.txt` (PowerShell, run by the observer):

   ```
   $p = "$env:USERPROFILE\Desktop","$env:USERPROFILE\Documents","$env:USERPROFILE\Pictures"
   Get-ChildItem $p -Recurse -File -ErrorAction SilentlyContinue |
     Select FullName,Length | Sort FullName | Export-Csv baseline.csv -NoTypeInformation
   Get-ChildItem $p -Recurse -File -ErrorAction SilentlyContinue |
     Get-FileHash -Algorithm SHA256 | Select Hash,Path | Export-Csv baseline-hash.csv -NoTypeInformation
   ```
   (Hashing a large Pictures folder takes minutes: do it during the welcome.)
3. Make a small **canary**: ten text files in `Documents\AurOS-session-canary`
   and note their hashes. If one changes, it is a data loss, even if nothing else is.
4. The **habit probe** (optional, with her consent): ask her to leave her email
   draft or a document open "as you normally would", unsaved. It tests whether
   anything restarts Windows without a word about saving (finding C-01).

**After T6, back in her Windows:** run the same two commands to `after.csv` and
`after-hash.csv`; compare them (`Compare-Object`). **Zero data loss** means:
every file in the baseline is present with the same size and hash, the canary is
intact, nothing is open and unsaved that was not unsaved before.

**One more check (finding I-01).** During T2 you create a file in AurOS's own
Documents (`from-aur.txt`, a sentence). After T6 look for it in Windows (it
should be in a folder "From AurOS": reverse Ferry, WP-27). Until WP-27 lands this is
a known gap: write the result, do not fail her for it, and tell the lead.

A data loss, any size, is an incident: stop recording, tell her plainly and
kindly, restore from the drive, and message the lead the same hour with the
sheet. It is a blocker for wave 1.

## 8. After the tasks (15 minutes, with tea)

Ask, in this order, and write her words:

1. "What was the hardest part?"
2. "Was there a moment you were worried?" (Where, and what were you afraid of?)
3. "Was there a moment you were pleased?"
4. "If I weren't here, would you have gone on?" (Where would she have stopped?)
5. "What would you tell a friend about it?"
6. After each task, earlier, on the printed five faces: "How easy was that?"
   (1 very hard, 5 very easy), ask while the memory is fresh.

Then: "Is there anything on your computer that looks different from how you left
it?" Then thank her, and give her the thank-you. Explain exactly what is on her
computer now, and how to go back (write it on the card, large).

## 9. Within a day

1. Fill the sheet from the recording; save recordings under the participant's
   number, not her name (`P01`, `P02`).
2. Write `docs/research/people-<date>.md`: one block per participant (pass or
   not and why, Clock A, aid levels, power attempts, data check) then the findings,
   each with its id from the walkthrough (or a new one), what happened, a
   screenshot name, and the owning stream (D website, B wizard, A2 staging, C
   desktop, W2 wishes, K help).
3. Tell the lead any POWER ATTEMPT or data loss the same day.
4. After all five: the pass count, and what changes in the plan. Repeat with five
   new people after the fixes.
