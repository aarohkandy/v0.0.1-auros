# Naming report: replacing "AurOS"

## Read this first

None of the five survivors is cleared. Each survived because no one found a decisive conflict, not because a full search came back clean. The shared web-search budget ran out mid-run. The trademark, product and language checks for Steady, Mason, Walnut and Amble were therefore mostly not run, and the language notes are the researchers' own judgment, not source-checked. Cottage got the fullest check. Domain checks (Shopify registry tool) did run for all five and are point-in-time.

Treat this ranking as "which names to investigate properly first", not "which names are safe".

## Ranked survivors

### 1. Cottage

- **Why it fits her:** A small, cozy, familiar place, spelled as it sounds, with no resemblance to any OS name. It leans toward real estate, so "Cottage OS" feels forced.
- **Trademark evidence:**
  - Searched Trademarkia aggregator pages only. Official USPTO, Justia and uspto.report pages returned 403.
  - Found live COTTAGE marks in unrelated fields: awnings (22), rose plants (31), ceramic tiles (19) and online lifestyle retail (35).
  - The only software-services COTTAGE mark was serial 88951309 (Cottage Tech LLC, class 42, filed 2020). The attacker confirmed it is dead, abandoned 26 Apr 2021. This removes the first researcher's "likely blocker".
  - COTTAGE AND MANOR LLC (2024) covers downloadable construction-advice software. It is a different mark in a different field.
  - The sidebar shows 22 class 9 and 81 class 42 "cottage" records that nobody could list.
  - EUIPO, UK IPO and WIPO were not checked.
- **Product/domain evidence:**
  - No OS, distro, senior product or AI assistant called Cottage was found.
  - Software users of the word: Cottage the ADU marketplace (now RenoFi), a short-term-rental platform (unverified, possibly dormant), and the defunct Cottage Software of Nashville.
  - cottage.com, cottage.app and getcottage.com are held by resellers. cottageos.com is available at $16, but "cottage-O-S dot com" is awkward on the phone.
- **Language check:**
  - Easy to say and spell for an English speaker. It is an established loanword in French, German and Italian.
  - No negative meaning in the listed languages (unverified).
  - One real issue: in UK slang, "cottage" and "cottaging" refer to public-toilet cruising. It is dated and mostly British, and a minor issue for a US-first launch.
- **Attacker's failed objections:**
  - The dead 2020 mark is not a bar.
  - The live COTTAGE registrations are all in unrelated classes.
  - The slang is dated British and marked non-vulgar in Wiktionary.
  - No OS or distro by that name exists.
- **Residual risk: medium.** It is a crowded dictionary word, so the mark is weak. A class 9/42 check of the official register is still missing. Domains are poor, and UK slang is a press risk.

### 2. Amble

- **Why it fits her:** It means to walk without hurry, which is the feeling we want for someone nervous about computers. It has two plain syllables and no odd spelling.
- **Trademark evidence:** Not searched. Nothing was seen either way.
- **Product/domain evidence:**
  - No product search ran.
  - amble.com, amble.app, amble.org, getamble.com and ambleos.com are all taken. Whether they are live businesses or parked is unknown.
  - Available: ambleos.org ($16), ambleos.net ($19) and ambletrail.com ($16).
- **Language check:**
  - On the phone, "Amble" and "Ample" are easy to confuse.
  - "Slow" could be read as "slow computer".
  - It resembles Spanish "amable" (kind), which is positive.
  - No negative meaning is known elsewhere (unverified).
- **Attacker's failed objections:** None found. The attacker had no search budget and found no evidence to justify a kill.
- **Residual risk: medium, unknown.** Fit is good, but the knockout is essentially blank. Every obvious domain is already held.

### 3. Mason

- **Why it fits her:** It sounds like a craftsman who builds things to last, plain and confident.
- **Trademark evidence:** Not searched. Expect many MASON marks, probably in classes 9, 35 and 42.
- **Product/domain evidence:**
  - mason.nvim is an active Neovim package manager (about 10.5k GitHub stars, 2026 release). It is a developer-tools neighbour, not an OS.
  - mason.com, getmason.com, masonos.com, mason.app and mason.org are all taken. masonos.com sits on a GoDaddy for-sale page.
  - Available: masonos.org ($16), getmason.net ($19).
- **Language check:**
  - Easy in English, but "Mason" vs "Jason" on a phone line is a minor risk.
  - It means freemason in Spanish, French, Portuguese and Italian, which can read as secret society to some older listeners.
  - No rude meaning in German, Hindi or Mandarin (unverified).
  - It suggests stone and bricks more than warmth.
- **Attacker's failed objections:** None established. The attacker could not search.
- **Residual risk: medium to high.** It is an unverified, crowded surname and word. No first-tier domain is obtainable at registration price.

### 4. Steady

- **Why it fits her:** It says the promise in her own words (it won't change on her, it won't break). Plain spelling and two syllables.
- **Trademark evidence:** Not verified. A class 9/42 conflict is likely given how many companies use the word, but this is memory, not search.
- **Product/domain evidence:**
  - getsteady.com is a live tribute and addiction-recovery site planning an app.
  - steady.com is parked (Digimedia).
  - SteadyIQ is a B2B income-verification tool, formerly the Steady income app.
  - Steadybit is a chaos-engineering SaaS.
  - steadyhq/steady.page is a membership and payments platform.
  - Microsoft's Windows SteadyState (2005 to 2011, discontinued) ties the word faintly to Windows, which the brief forbids.
  - steady.com, steady.app, steady.org, steadyos.com, steadyos.org and getsteady.com are taken.
  - Available: steadyos.app ($20), steadyos.net ($19).
- **Language check:**
  - Easy to say, hear and spell.
  - "Going steady" (dating) and "unsteady on your feet" could sound patronizing or suggest fall risk.
  - Nothing negative is known in the other languages (unverified).
- **Attacker's failed objections:** None established. No registered mark or Steady-named OS or distro was found, and SteadyState is long dead.
- **Residual risk: high.** Several live software and fintech users, a Windows-adjacent history, and no clean domain.

### 5. Walnut

- **Why it fits her:** It is the wood of the console TV and the good desk in her day, so it feels familiar, solid and unhurried. Easy to spell and say.
- **Trademark evidence:** Not searched on any register.
- **Product/domain evidence:**
  - **Confirmed conflict:** walnut.io is a live, current product from Walnut Ltd (© 2026). It is an AI-based buyer-enablement SaaS platform with an "AI buyer agent" feature.
  - That is identical-name software with AI, in the territory of classes 9 and 42. It sells B2B demos, not consumer desktops, but a lawyer would cite it.
  - walnut.com, walnut.app and walnut.org are taken.
  - walnut.app is parked on a domain marketplace.
  - walnutos.com is parked at a $1,000 minimum offer.
  - getwalnut.com was reported "taken" by one researcher, but the attacker found it does not resolve.
  - Available: walnutos.org ($16), getwalnut.org ($16), plus .net variants.
  - An Indian expense-tracking app also named Walnut is unverified.
- **Language check:**
  - Easy to say.
  - "Wall-nut" vs "Wallnut" is a plausible misspelling.
  - "Nuts" is English slang for crazy.
  - German Walnuss is positive. Nothing negative was found elsewhere (unverified).
- **Attacker's failed objections:** The attacker found the live AI product but judged it short of a kill. No OS was found, and the goods differ (B2B sales demos vs a consumer desktop).
- **Residual risk: high.** Confirmed same-name AI software, plus weak domains.

## Recommended top 5, in order

1. **Cottage:** The best-evidenced survivor, with its one software-class mark dead, and the warmest fit, but the UK slang and weak domains need weighing.
2. **Amble:** The best emotional fit for a nervous first-time user, but it is essentially unsearched, so verify before falling in love.
3. **Mason:** Sturdy and plain, but crowded, with the freemason connotation and no good domain.
4. **Steady:** Says the right promise, but several live software users and a Windows-adjacent history make it risky.
5. **Walnut:** Warm and familiar, but a live AI software product shares the exact name.

## What the owner should do before committing

1. **Finish the knockout, free.** Search the USPTO register directly for the top three in classes 9, 42 and 38. Also search EUIPO, UK IPO and WIPO, the app stores, and GitHub. For Cottage, look up the 22 class 9 and 81 class 42 records nobody could list. Do this before anything costs money.
2. **A paid clearance search is optional, but worth it for the single name you actually pick.** A professional report covers common-law use and foreign registers that free tools miss. It is more valuable than any of the work above if one name is clearly favored.
3. **File an intent-to-use application, classes 9 and 42, once the name survives step 1.** The USPTO fee is currently about $350 per class (check the current schedule). This reserves a priority date but is not clearance. File only after a search, since a filing can be refused or opposed.
4. **Buy the domain last, and cheaply.** Register an available domain such as cottageos.com ($16), or an .org or .net equivalent, once you are fairly committed. Do not pay aftermarket prices for cottage.com or similar before clearance. Note that "name dot com" is what she will type, and every top-tier domain for these names is held by someone else.
5. Keep "AurOS" strictly internal until you rename. Do not launch publicly under it.
