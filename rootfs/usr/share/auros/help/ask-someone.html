Who do I ask?

Ask someone you trust who knows computers: a son, a daughter, a grandchild, a neighbour or a friend. Show them what is on the screen.

The people who make AurOS have no phone line, and they will never phone you.

What to tell them

  • What you were trying to do.
  • What the screen said or showed, in your own words.
  • A photo of the screen, taken with a phone, helps a lot.

Don’t give anyone your passwords, bank details or gift cards, even a helper. They never need them.

For the person who helps

If the computer keeps going wrong, press Settings at the bottom of the screen, then Save a report for whoever helps you, and plug in a memory stick. It saves one small file with no passwords in it. Give the stick to your helper.

The AurOS website has a page for helpers, and a page to print. Both are free. Search for AurOS on the internet from another computer or a phone.

If AurOS itself is at fault, your helper can write to the people who make it, on the AurOS project page. Volunteers answer, usually within a few days. There is no promise of how fast. Their message is public, so it must have no passwords in it.

If it cannot wait

  • Your photos and papers matter more than AurOS. If the computer becomes very slow and makes clicking noises, copy them to a memory stick first. Then ask.
  • To go back to Windows, see Going back to Windows.
  • If someone has got into your computer or your accounts, see If you already let someone in.

To finish, press Close this at the bottom of the screen.
