AurOS will never call you

Nobody from AurOS will ever phone you, email you, or show you a message asking you to call a number.

AurOS will never ask you:

  • to call a phone number;
  • to let someone connect to your computer, or to get a program so they can “see your screen”;
  • for card numbers, passwords, bank details or gift cards;
  • for your passwords, or for a code sent to your phone.

Anyone who asks for any of these is not from AurOS, Microsoft or your bank, whatever they say.

A page that says your computer has a problem

A website may fill the screen with a warning, sometimes with a loud noise. It says your computer has a virus or is locked, and gives a number to call. It is fake. A web page cannot tell whether your computer has a virus.

  1. Don’t call the number, and don’t press anything on the page.
  2. Press Help at the bottom left of the screen, then Get me out of this page. That bar always works, whatever the page says. The sound goes off.
  3. Press Close this page. The page is gone. (Pressing the Esc key three times, quickly, does the same.)
  4. If the noise won’t go, press Turn off at the bottom right, then Turn it off. Switch it on again later.
  5. If the page comes back when you open Internet, do the same again, and ask someone you trust to look.

A program that lets someone use your computer

If AurOS says “Before you install this”, the program lets someone far away use your computer. Scammers ask people to install it. Press Don’t install it. Press I called someone I know only if YOU called a person you know, and they asked you to.

A phone call about your computer

People call pretending to be from Microsoft, AurOS, your internet company, the police or your bank. They say there is a problem with your computer or your account. Hang up. A real company won’t mind. If you are worried, call the company back yourself, on a number you already have, such as the one on your bank card or your bill.

If you already let someone in

It happens to careful people every day, and it isn’t your fault. Act now:

  1. Press Turn off, then Turn it off. That cuts them off.
  2. Call your bank on the number on the back of your card. Tell them what happened.
  3. From another phone or computer, change the passwords for your email and your bank.
  4. Ask someone you trust to help you check this computer before you use it for banking again. You can also report it to the police.

When you have finished reading, press Close this at the bottom of the screen.
